## Documented economic terms

**DOCUMENTED FACT — exact Appendix A pp.5-8:** Reference Volume is USD notional
of all reported event-contract transactions on Gemini Titan during reported
November 1-30, 2026 trade dates. Include fractional quantities, all categories,
payout types, active/closed contracts, counterparties and submission channels,
including a successor under the same market designation. Gemini Olympus clears
the trades. Count quantities times USD 1 or another settlement value identified
for that contract by the source. Trade prices and premium do not enter the metric.
The figure is computed to cents as reported, without adjustment, sampling or
estimation. **YES requires strictly more than USD 100,000,000; equality is NO.**
Ordinary YES-position payout is USD 1 for YES, USD 0 for NO. These ordinary payouts
do not describe void/refund or Rule 5.2 allocations. The market-price tick is a
separate concept from Reference Volume precision.

Other venues, non-US Gemini venues and Gemini's digital-asset, perpetuals,
custody and other businesses are excluded. The permitted publication list is
exhaustive. Do not use earnings/investor/press cumulative numbers, UTC-day volume,
account metrics, reward programs, partner/broker/aggregator figures, annualized,
rounded or premium statistics, or multi-venue totals. A similar metric name does
not make those figures eligible.

**NORMALIZATION INTERPRETATION:** local entity/metric/source identifiers are
labels, not exchange-issued IDs. Canonical `100000000` represents the threshold;
`100000000.00` is the same mathematical value but not a valid canonical v1 string.
Synthetic `99999999.99` / `100000000` / `100000000.01` witnesses use the filing's
cent scale, not an invented universal Gemini quantity increment. Gemini's API
documents decimal strings but do not independently define every volume field as
USD notional or a universal one-dollar multiplier. Attribute that method to the
ProphetX filing.

## Reference period and temporal derivation

**DOCUMENTED FACT:** reported trade-date attribution controls, including
weekends. The filing describes the then-current day as 17:00:00 ET on the
preceding day through 16:59:59 ET on the named day, and describes the resulting
execution-clock envelope as expected. It is not an unconditional UTC timestamp
filter.

**NORMALIZATION INTERPRETATION:** Temporal with `America/New_York` derives:

| Expected boundary                               | Local time                | Instant                |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| First date opens                                | October 31, 17:00 EDT     | `2026-10-31T21:00:00Z` |
| First date's final written second               | November 1, 16:59:59 EST  | `2026-11-01T21:59:59Z` |
| Last date's final written second / last trading | November 30, 16:59:59 EST | `2026-11-30T21:59:59Z` |
| Following 17:00 boundary                        | November 30, 17:00 EST    | `2026-11-30T22:00:00Z` |

The half-open envelope through the last row spans **721 hours**; the first trade
date spans **25 hours** across fall-back. A fixed UTC offset would be wrong.
Half-open subsecond treatment after the final written second is an interpretation,
not additional rule text. Tests verify the derivation; they do not establish
reported trade-date attribution from execution timestamps.

**DOCUMENTED FACT:** expiration / Determination Cutoff is 17:00 ET on the second
business day following the period. Settlement is due no later than one business
day following expiration, except review; it is not mandated at one fixed instant.

**UNVERIFIED / UNKNOWN:** no general settlement business-day definition was
established in the complete Rulebook definitions/usages or Terms. Under an
explicit Monday-Friday calendar assumption with December 1/2 open, the successive
days are Tuesday December 1 and Wednesday December 2; candidate cutoff is
`2026-12-02T22:00:00Z`, and candidate ordinary settlement deadline December 3.
This is conditional arithmetic, not an established ProphetX calendar rule. The
real draft does not bind these instants. No calendar engine was added.

## Sources, computation and fallback

**DOCUMENTED FACT — exact Appendix A pp.6-7:** apply the following order **for
each trade date**, keeping retrieved reports and retrieval times and disclosing
substitutions:

1. Market Data: sum `contracts_traded` across all rows, including closed and zero
   rows. Do not separately add `block_volume`; open interest and prices are unused.
2. If primary data is missing for that date by cutoff, Trade Data: sum `Quantity`
   across every transaction record.
3. If still missing, official hourly volume: sum only rows with a one-element
   `categoryPath`, across the UTC hours of that trade date. Descendants are already
   included and must not be added again. UTC-day endpoint totals are excluded.

The three methods derive from the same trades and are expected to agree. The
filing treats differences as material only when the alternative changes the
binary outcome. It does not simply direct a conflicting lower-tier observation
to override a complete higher tier. Inability to determine Reference Volume with
a settlement disruption can invoke Rule 5.2. An invented conflict-to-review rule
would not settle that question.

Gemini's developer docs corroborate descendant inclusion, completed UTC-hour
data and decimal strings. They describe missing-hour 404 and invalid/unavailable
canonical-data 503 behavior, not a permission to invent zeroes for absent data.
Successful zero-volume hours need not have synthetic rows. The filing, not a
separately verified Gemini dictionary, supplies the CSV field instructions.

**CLAIMLINT MODEL LIMITATION:** v1 chooses a whole-period scalar from one source.
It neither composes source tiers per date nor verifies completeness, multipliers,
category aggregation or excluded-statistic provenance. Trusted manual preparation
can compute a correct scalar for one world, but cannot make global fallback
equivalent to the contract's date-by-date rule. The synthetic witness has primary
USD 60m on November 1, missing primary/secondary USD 50m on November 2 and 28
complete zero dates. The contractual aggregate is USD 110m (YES); choosing an
incomplete whole-source subtotal of USD 60m gives NO. Relabelling that subtotal
as a complete monthly observation is the semantic distortion the gate prevents.

## Corrections, disruption, void and finality

**DOCUMENTED FACT — exact Appendix A pp.7-8:** compute once from data published
as of cutoff. Earlier corrections are reflected. Later published corrections,
including Regulation 16.06 corrections, do not change settlement or cause
resettlement. This ordinary rule is expressly subject to Rule 5.2 in the common
provisions; it is not unconditional finality under all circumstances.

**NORMALIZATION INTERPRETATION:** inclusive publication at the exact whole-second
cutoff follows the as-of wording and exclusion of after-cutoff corrections.
Subsecond publication precision is not established. The scalar witness tests one
second before, exactly at, and one second after. Its availability equals
publication unless the test explicitly exhibits the separate retrieval limitation.
The filing records retrieval times but frames the cutoff in publication terms;
the core also requires availability to the evaluator by cutoff. These clocks
cannot silently be equated for every real historical collection.

**DOCUMENTED FACT:** ceased/restricted/materially changed reporting, changed
meaning/units, lost trade-date reporting or inclusion of non-Titan activity require
the next available means for the same Titan data. Another publication of the same
data may be consulted. If no means remains and the outcome is not unequivocally
determined, or the outcome cannot reasonably be determined under the absence
clause, orders are void and collateral returned. Partial missing data does not
automatically imply void when the outcome is already determined.

**CLAIMLINT MODEL LIMITATION:** void is a defined, normally terminal disposition,
distinct from NO, an undefined contract, and a pending review. It should carry an
explicit collateral-return disposition rather than a fake binary payout. It is
a sufficiently general candidate primitive, but adding it alone would not resolve
the other gates. No VOID mapping or extension was implemented for this case.

**DOCUMENTED FACT — Rulebook v4.0 Rule 5.2(a)-(f), printed p.40:** settlement is
final once effected subject to the Exchange's power to reverse, amend or resettle
after a materially disruptive event. ProphetX DCO decides reliable information
and may use last-traded-price allocation, delay, void/cancel, interpret uncertain
terms or take other permitted action. Combined payout is bounded. Exchange
records are conclusive; Rule 5.2 determinations are not appealable under that
rule. Rules 2.8-2.9 preserve concurrent emergency powers, including term,
expiration and price changes; Rule 4.6 supplies trade review. Rule 4.7 addresses
order-system disputes, not a substitute settlement appeal. Chapter 12 general
arbitration is a separate legal route.

**CLAIMLINT MODEL LIMITATION:** a named `REVIEW_REQUIRED` route could identify
Rule 5.2 for human action. It cannot calculate the available allocations, encode
the declared triggers/judgment, or represent a later authorized change to an
effected settlement. Sending every source issue to review would overstate the
terms and hide both ordinary computation and explicit void behavior. VOID plus
per-date fallback still would not faithfully represent this lifecycle. A broader
rule/action model is outside this task and v1's narrow scope.
